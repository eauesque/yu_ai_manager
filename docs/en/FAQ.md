## Support, Bugs, Feature Requests

**Q: I can't create a PR.**
A: We don't accept PRs. That's it.

**Q: Please add this feature.**
A: We've provided the Extensions API and documentation. Either build it yourself, or ask an AI agent to "make an extension referring to https://github.com/eauesque/yu_ai_manager". If you can't do that, this software is too advanced for you yet.

**Q: There's a bug. Please fix it.**
A: Paste the log on GitHub. I'll look at it if I feel like it. If you want it fixed immediately, give the error log to an AI agent — it will be faster and more reliable than me. Either way, your problem is for you to solve.

**Q: I don't understand the specification.**
A: Stop lying. All documentation is provided. Read it. If you can't read it, have an AI read it for you. If you can't even do that, grow a bit and come back later.

**Q: I don't even know what I don't understand.**
A: Even just thinking loosely about it or reading the manual might bring something to mind.

**Q: Please provide support.**
A: We made, you decide. Are you confused about something?

**Q: If you don't fix it, I'll be in trouble.**
A: Then be in trouble. Your trouble and my labor are separate matters. Bring compensation and come back. That's how society works.

**Q: Will you do it if I provide compensation?**
A: Not unless it's worth my effort. Obviously.

**Q: Just fix it 💢**
A: Are you a beggar or an outlaw? When making a request, at least say "Would it be possible to...". Didn't you learn that in school?

## Responsibility, Ownership, Property Rights

**Q: You're irresponsible.**
A: We made, you decide. It was written that way from the start. You just didn't read it.

**Q: I suffered damages using this software. It's the fault of an incompetent, utterly irresponsible author.**
A: That's not how it is. You made the decision to use software created by an author who just made it as a side project. We made, you decide.

**Q: You made me fail.**
A: Don't blame others for what you did. If you regret it, blame yourself. If it was something you decided to do yourself, you should always be able to come to terms with it.

**Q: The community should decide. Development direction should be discussed by everyone.**
A: Didn't you learn about property rights in elementary school? The road can be used by anyone, but it belongs to the country. It doesn't belong to you. Copyright is retained by us — we're only granting you usage rights. To begin with, you're using the software for free; there's no trade relationship at all. Insects have no right to decide design. Bring me the legal basis for interfering with design. If you can't, just use it silently or don't use it.

**Q: Listen to what users say.**
A: Are you my paid client? No. What obligation do I have to listen to demands from someone I'm not in business with? Fork the project and create your own. That's what the MIT license means. The rights given to you are limited to three options: "use," "don't use," or "fork."

**Q: Aren't you interested in discussions or building a community?**
A: No. It's not sustainable. A person with a full-time job has no capacity to manage a community on top of that.

**Q: I don't like the development direction.**
A: Too bad. Fork it. That's all.

**Q: Isn't this a license violation?**
A: Research it first. Read THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES.txt. If you still want to complain, bring a lawyer. I'm prepared.

**Q: Isn't open source supposed to be for everyone?**
A: Look up the meaning of "public." Roads, parks, libraries — anyone can use them, but they're owned by the government. They don't belong to you. "Public" means "the owner manages it while allowing broad use," not "ownership is distributed among the masses." Open source is the same. Copyright is retained by the author — we're only granting usage rights. Being able to use something is different from owning it. If you can't make that distinction, study law a bit more and come back.

## Technical Questions

**Q: I wrote a bug report in Japanese on GitHub but got no response.**
A: Bug reports on GitHub are in English only. Discussions are closed. We only accept bug reports. Write in English or don't expect a response.

**Q: Can I change the development direction by appealing emotionally?**
A: Bug responses are handled by an AI agent, not a human. Emotional arguments, appeals to guilt, and social pressure don't work on language models. State technical facts clearly in English. Otherwise, you won't get a meaningful response.

**Q: Why do you use Danbooru tags?**
A: Danbooru tags are the de facto standard for tagging AI-generated images. Stable Diffusion, NovelAI, countless LoRAs — nearly all major image generation models were trained on datasets tagged in Danbooru format. This software uses Danbooru tags because it's the industry standard, not because it endorses or supports the Danbooru project itself. If you have complaints about the tag system, take them to the entire AI image generation ecosystem, not this tool.

**Q: On macOS, it says "unverified developer" and won't launch.**
A: That's correct. Code signing costs money. This software is free. Right-click the app, select "Open," and click "Open" again in the dialog. Just the first time. If that's too much trouble, figure out how to pay for the certificate yourself.

**Q: Windows SmartScreen is blocking the installer.**
A: Expected. SmartScreen's reputation is built on distribution track record, not capability. Click "More info" → "Run anyway." If you can't judge that for yourself, you should use friendlier software.
